About the data

What we check, and what a match means.

What we check

  • Our research: 1,724 ships, 1,007 companies and 25 people in the shadow-fleet network, 8 officially listed companies from our semiconductor chip research and the listed companies and intermediaries in our lobbying research. A snapshot from the VeilTrace build day, 25 September 2026. The shadow-fleet map is rebuilt every night and now shows 1,747 ships and 1,037 companies.
  • United States: the Consolidated Screening List, which includes the Treasury's OFAC sanctions list and Commerce export-control lists.
  • United Kingdom: the UK Sanctions List.
  • European Union: the consolidated list of persons, groups and entities subject to EU financial sanctions.
  • United Nations: the Security Council consolidated list.
  • OpenSanctions: consolidated sanctions data from 95 sources worldwide, refreshed nightly from OpenSanctions' free bulk data. CC BY-NC 4.0.
  • Connections: owners, managers and linked companies from the official sanctions lists (via OpenSanctions' relationship data), and US lobbying from Lobbying Disclosure Act filings (lda.gov API) for a tracked list of companies, both refreshed nightly. Lobbyists are named only from their own disclosures. No filing found is not proof of no influence.

How matching works

Names are compared after removing accents, punctuation and company endings such as LLC, Ltd or OOO, so small spelling differences still match. A seven-digit number, with or without "IMO" in front, is treated as a ship's IMO number and matched exactly. Names written only in non-Latin script cannot be matched; type them in Latin letters.

What a match means

A name match is a possible match, not proof. Ships and companies share names. Open the numbered source before you publish. This site describes documented conduct and makes no legal findings. It is not legal advice.

Known limits

  • EU bans on individual ships sit in an annex to Regulation 833/2014, not in the EU financial sanctions file. Those ship listings appear under "In our research", not under the official EU list.
  • The US Consolidated Screening List often gives no listing date; the table then shows a dash.
  • The US, UK and UN files have no link per record, so "List" opens the official list page.
  • Our research is a snapshot and is not refreshed nightly.

How the lists stay current

Every night, around midnight New York time, the four official lists are downloaded and checked. A list that fails to download, changes shape, or shrinks by more than a fifth keeps yesterday's copy, and the page says "Some lists may be out of date". The other lists still update.

How the graphs update

The shadow-fleet map and the influence network are rebuilt every night from the official lists, the OFAC and EU Official Journal exports from OpenSanctions, and US lobbying filings. The Updates page lists what changed each day. If one of those sources fails or shrinks by more than a fifth, that night's graphs keep its previous copy. The Updates page marks the source unavailable and reports no changes from it, so a failed download is never shown as a delisting. The research snapshot of 25 September 2026 stays fixed for search and the dossiers.

Ask and your questions

Ask, the chat panel, sends your question and data from this site to an AI model (Claude Haiku 5.5, through OpenRouter). Each request tells OpenRouter to use only model providers that don't collect user data. The sentences of its answer then go, with your question, to a relevance check (TypeSafe), which removes any that don't bear on the question. Under its terms, TypeSafe does not use what it is sent for training. Undercurrent stores no conversation: there are no accounts, the conversation lives only in your browser tab and is gone when you close the panel, and the server logs no message text.

Corrections and removal requests

We name companies and people only as official sanctions lists, government documents or, for lobbyists, their own US lobbying filings name them. A company known to us only from other sources is shown by a description, such as "unlisted ship manager", not by its name.

To ask for a correction or removal, open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so do not include sensitive personal data. We aim to respond within 7 days. While we check a request, we remove disputed content that does not come from an official source.
